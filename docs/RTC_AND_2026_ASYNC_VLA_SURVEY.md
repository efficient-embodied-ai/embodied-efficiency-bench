## 1. Exact Mechanics of Real-Time Chunking (RTC, `arXiv:2506.07339`, NeurIPS 2025)

### 1.1 Exact Notation & Two-Sided Inequality (Section 3, `neurips_2025.tex`)
* **Prediction Horizon ($H$):** Length of the action chunk $\mathbf{A}_t = [\mathbf{a}_t, \mathbf{a}_{t+1}, \dots, \mathbf{a}_{t+H-1}]$ generated by policy $\pi(\mathbf{A}_t \mid \mathbf{o}_t)$ in a single forward pass (e.g., $H = 50$).
* **Execution Horizon ($s$, called $K$ in our benchmark):** Number of actions from each chunk actually executed before switching to the next chunk ($s \le H$).
* **Controller Period ($\Delta t$), Model Latency ($\delta$), and Inference Delay ($d$):**
  $$d := \lfloor \delta / \Delta t \rfloor \quad (\text{or } \lceil L / \Delta t \rceil \text{ discrete steps elapsed between capturing } \mathbf{o}_t \text{ and receiving } \mathbf{A}_t)$$
* **The Fundamental RTC Inequality (`neurips_2025.tex` line 166):**
  $$d \le s \le H - d \qquad \left(\text{which requires } d \le \lfloor H / 2 \rfloor\right)$$
  * **Why $s \ge d$:** The GPU can only compute one chunk at a time, and each chunk takes $d$ control steps to compute. Therefore, you cannot switch chunks any faster than once every $d$ steps.
  * **Why $s \le H - d$:** If the background thread starts computing the next chunk after $s$ steps have elapsed, the robot must keep executing the *current* chunk for $d$ more steps while the GPU runs (reaching index $s + d - 1$). For the current chunk of length $H$ not to run out of actions before the new chunk arrives, we must have $s + d \le H \iff s \le H - d$.

### 1.2 How RTC's Background Thread Works (`Algorithm 1` in `arXiv:2506.07339`)
In RTC (`Section 4.3`), the user specifies a **minimum desired execution horizon $s_{\min}$**, and the actual execution horizon on each chunk is:
$$s = \max(d, s_{\min})$$
This produces two operating regimes:
1. **Paced Asynchronous Mode ($s_{\min} > d \implies s = s_{\min}$):**
   * Used in Physical Intelligence's real-world $\pi_{0.5}$ experiments (`Table 4`: $H = 50$, $s_{\min} = 25$, while $d \approx 6\text{ to }16$).
   * The background inference thread waits until $s_{\min}$ steps have executed, then runs inference for $d$ steps and swaps in the new chunk.
2. **Back-to-Back Continuous Mode ($s_{\min} \le d \implies s = d$):**
   * Used in dynamic simulation benchmarks (`Kinetix Figure 5 Right`, where $s_{\min} = 1 \implies s = \max(d, 1) = d$).
   * The moment one chunk finishes computing on the GPU, the background thread **immediately** starts computing the next chunk without waiting. A new chunk is swapped in every $d$ steps ($s = d$).

### 1.3 The Three Regions of Soft Masking in RTC (`Eq. 5` & `Figure 3`)
When generating a new chunk of length $H$ after $s$ actions of the previous chunk $\mathbf{A}_{\text{prev}}$ have executed, $\mathbf{A}_{\text{prev}}$ has $H - s$ remaining actions. RTC partitions the new chunk indices $i \in \{0, \dots, H-1\}$ into three regions with weight vector $\mathbf{W}_i$:

$$\mathbf{W}_i = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } 0 \le i < d \quad \text{(Region 1: Frozen Committed Prefix)} \\ c_i \frac{e^{c_i}-1}{e-1} & \text{if } d \le i < H-s \quad \text{(Region 2: Softly Blended Overlap)} \\ 0 & \text{if } H-s \le i < H \quad \text{(Region 3: Newly Generated Tail)} \end{cases}, \qquad c_i = \frac{H-s-i}{H-s-d+1}$$

1. **Region 1 ($0 \le i < d$, Frozen Prefix, $\mathbf{W}_i = 1$):** These $d$ steps execute on the physical robot *while* the GPU is computing the new chunk. When the new chunk finishes $d$ steps later, the controller jumps straight to index $i = d$ of the new chunk, so indices $0 \dots d-1$ act purely as the frozen boundary condition for flow inpainting.
2. **Region 2 ($d \le i < H - s$, Soft Overlap, $\mathbf{W}_i \in (0, 1)$):** Actions from the previous chunk exist here, but inference finishes *before* they execute. Exponentially decaying weights pull the early post-switch steps ($i \ge d$) smoothly toward the previous chunk while allowing later steps to adapt to the new camera frame.
3. **Region 3 ($H - s \le i < H$, Free Tail, $\mathbf{W}_i = 0$):** Lies beyond the end of the previous chunk; generated freely from the new observation.

## 2. Verified Compute Overhead of Test-Time RTC (`IT-RTC`)

Because test-time RTC (`IT-RTC`) computes a vector-Jacobian product (`vjp`) via backpropagation through the velocity network $\mathbf{v}_\pi$ at every denoising step ($\sim 3\times$ denoising FLOPs), its measured latency overhead across papers is:
* **Original RTC Paper (`arXiv:2506.07339`, Table 1, $\pi_{0.5}$ on RTX 4090, 5 denoising steps):**
  * SigLIP Vision Encoder: `18 ms` (both)
  * Gemma-2B LLM Prefill: `44 ms` (both)
  * 5 Flow Denoising Steps (Gemma-300M Action Expert): `14 ms` (`2.8 ms/step`, Vanilla) vs. **`35 ms` (`7.0 ms/step`, `IT-RTC`, $2.5\times$ slower denoising)**
  * **Total GPU Latency:** `76 ms` (Vanilla) $\to$ **`97 ms` (`IT-RTC`, `+21 ms` / $+27.6\%$ overhead)**.
* **TT-RTC Paper (`arXiv:2512.05964`, Section V-B, $\pi_{0.6}$ on H100 server):**
  * `TT-RTC`: **`108 ms`** ($d \approx 5$) vs. `IT-RTC`: **`135 ms`** ($d \approx 7$, **`+27 ms` / $+25.0\%$ overhead**).
* **VLASH Paper (`arXiv:2512.01031v2`, Table II, $\pi_{0.5}$ with `torch.compile` + CUDA Graphs, 1 camera):**
  * **H100:** `23.2 ms` (Vanilla/VLASH) vs. **`48.4 ms` (`IT-RTC`, $2.09\times$ slower)**
  * **RTX 5090:** `29.4 ms` (Vanilla/VLASH) vs. **`49.6 ms` (`IT-RTC`, $1.69\times$ slower)**
  * **RTX 4090:** `32.3 ms` (Vanilla/VLASH) vs. **`54.2 ms` (`IT-RTC`, $1.68\times$ slower)**
* **Agouzoul Unified Benchmark (`arXiv:2605.08168`, Table 2, SmolVLA on RTX 3090):**
  * Naive: `405.2 ms`, `TT-RTC`: `402.7 ms` ($0.99\times$), **`IT-RTC`: `469.7 ms` ($1.16\times$, `+64.5 ms` slower)**.
